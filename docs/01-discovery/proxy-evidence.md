# Craft BD — Secondary Proxy Evidence

## 1. Purpose

This document records external evidence that can support or challenge Craft BD's current problem hypotheses before primary user interviews are completed.

This evidence is **secondary research**.

It must not be reported as primary customer or artisan interview data.

---

# 2. Evidence Sources

The current evidence is based on:

- Bangladesh Foreign Trade Institute (BFTI) research on the handicraft sector
- Palli Karma-Sahayak Foundation (PKSF) initiatives related to handicrafts and microentrepreneur products
- Published reporting on Bangladesh's social-commerce and e-commerce ecosystem
- Existing digital craft platforms reviewed during competitor analysis

---

# 3. Handicraft Sector Evidence

BFTI's research on the Bangladesh handicraft sector identified multiple challenges through stakeholder interviews and focus-group discussions.

Relevant challenges included:

- Lack of promotion of handicraft products
- Weak country branding
- Inadequate market linkage
- Limited research and design capability
- Infrastructure and supply-side constraints
- Financing constraints
- Shortage of skilled artisans

Only some of these problems can realistically be addressed through Craft BD.

---

# 4. Software-Relevant Problems

Based on secondary research, the following areas appear relevant to a digital product:

| Problem Area | Evidence | Craft BD Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Limited promotion | Supported | Product and artisan discovery |
| Weak market linkage | Supported | Digital connection between buyers and artisans |
| Weak branding / presentation | Supported | Structured artisan and craft presentation |
| Limited product knowledge | Partially supported | Craft story and product information |
| Customer trust in informal digital commerce | Partially supported | Structured product and seller records |
| Informal order communication | Plausible but not yet directly validated | Custom-order workflow |

---

# 5. Industry Direction Evidence

PKSF's 2026 microenterprise promotion initiative discussed several approaches relevant to product presentation and market linkage, including:

- Region-based product branding
- QR code-based packaging and labeling
- Production-process displays
- Video storytelling
- Digital promotion and marketing
- Product knowledge presentation
- Certification support
- Stronger market linkage

This is useful evidence that product storytelling, structured product information, QR-linked information, and market linkage are relevant ideas within Bangladesh's current microenterprise ecosystem.

However, this does not prove that Craft BD's specific implementation will solve a customer problem.

---

# 6. Social-Commerce Evidence

Bangladesh has a significant Facebook-based commerce ecosystem.

Published reporting has documented concerns including:

- Delivery problems
- Incorrect products
- Low-quality products
- Weak seller accountability
- Customer trust concerns
- Dependence on direct communication

These findings indicate that structured digital commerce and trustworthy seller/product information may provide value.

However, this evidence is broader than the handicraft sector and should not automatically be treated as proof of Craft BD-specific requirements.

---

# 7. Existing Solution Evidence

Existing platforms demonstrate that several Craft BD capabilities are already common or partially available.

Examples include:

- Online product discovery
- Product listings
- Maker / artisan representation
- Online ordering
- Delivery
- Seller vetting
- Maker stories

Therefore:

**Generic online shopping is not Craft BD's validated core problem.**

---

# 10. Still-Unvalidated Core Area

The most important unresolved hypothesis is:

**Structured Custom Ordering**

The current Craft BD concept assumes that customers and artisans may benefit from:

Customer Request  
→ Structured Specifications  
→ Artisan Review  
→ Quotation  
→ Customer Approval  
→ Production  
→ Status Updates  
→ Delivery

Secondary research does not provide sufficient evidence to confirm that this workflow solves a significant handicraft-specific user problem.

Direct primary research is required.

# 12. Research Integrity Statement

No simulated interview, generated persona, synthetic participant, or secondary source should be counted as a real customer or artisan interview.

Synthetic data may be used only for:

- Template testing
- Requirement-analysis practice
- Prototype testing
- Demo data
- Workflow simulation

Any final claim based on primary research must be supported by actual participants.

---

# 13. Primary Research Still Required

Recommended minimum pilot before finalizing the Craft BD problem statement:

### Customers

At least 3 real customer interviews.

### Artisans / Sellers

At least 2 real artisan or craft-seller interviews.

A larger sample should be used where practical.

The purpose is exploratory problem validation, not statistical representation of the entire Bangladesh handicraft market.

---

# 14. Current Discovery Conclusion

Secondary evidence supports the existence of challenges around handicraft promotion, market linkage, branding, and product presentation.

It also indicates that QR-based information, storytelling, and production-process presentation are relevant approaches currently being explored within Bangladesh's microenterprise ecosystem.

However, Craft BD's strongest proposed workflow — structured custom ordering — remains a hypothesis.

Therefore, the project should maintain a **provisional problem statement** until real customer and artisan interviews are completed.
